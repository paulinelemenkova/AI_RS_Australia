% Abstract (Do not insert blank lines, i.e. \\) 
\abstract{Coastal regions are heterogeneous areas with a complex landscape patterns that reflect the interplay between natural ecosystems and human activities. The environmental settings of such areas reflect complex links between the terrestrial and marine natural ecosystems. On the other hand, coastal lands experience high anthropogenic pressure such as intensive urban construction, industrial and agriculture activities, marine transport systems and harbours. Monitoring the dynamics of the coastal areas is essential for effective land management, natural resource planning, and urban development. Remote sensing (RS) data, such as satellite images, present a robust, reliable and cost-efficient information source to monitor coastal landscapes through time series analysis which enable to compare and detect changes. Land cover change detection using RS data processing is crucial for coastal monitoring. However, traditional tools of Geographic Information System (GIS) have restricted capabilities and limitations in speed and accuracy of RS data processing. This paper evaluates the potential of using Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) approach for classification of Landsat satellite imagery to identify land cover types in a coastal area around Cheetham Wetlands, Port Phillip Bay, Australia. The scripting approach of Geographic Resources Analysis Support System (GRASS) GIS uses AI-based methods of image analysis to accurately discriminate land cover types. Four ML algorithms were applied for supervised classification using "r.learn.train" module that employs the Scikit-Learn library of Python. The algorithms include: 1) Random Forest (RF), 2) Support Vector Machine (SVM), 3) ANN-based approach using Multi-layer Perceptron (MLP) Classifier; and 4) Decision Tree Classifier (DTC). The tested methods of AI demonstrated excellent results of image classification with the highest overall accuracy that exceeds 98\% reached by the SVM and RF scenarios. The presented scripting approach of GRASS GIS accurately detected changes in land cover types in southern Victoria over the period of 2013-2024. The use AI and ML algorithms offers effective solutions for coastal monitoring by analysis of change detection using multi-temporal RS data. The demonstrated method has potential applications in coastal and wetland monitoring, environmental analysis and urban planning based on Earth observation.}

\section{Study Area}

The research is focused on the are around Melbourne - the second largest city on the Australian continent, and encompasses the Port Phillip Bay. The exact zone of the study area is limited by the coordinates from 143°01'33.38" E to 143°08'03.62"E longitude and from 38°32'14.93"S to 36°24'10.94"S latitude, which is located in the southern part of the state of Victoria, Figure \ref{fig01}. The state of Victoria comprises 79 municipalities with a total population exceeding 6.7 million in 2023, which makes it the second-most-populated state (after New South Wales) in Australia \cite{ABS}. Such population density increases anthropogenic pressure on the natural environmental of coastal areas. On the other hand, there is a strong link between the coastal environment and quality of life for population living in this area which illustrates dense human-nature interactions \cite{LIAO2023111158,HUANG2020134680}. 
 
 \begin{figure}[H]
    \begin{center}
	\hspace{-35pt}\includegraphics[width=14.0cm]{Fig_01.jpg}
    \end{center}
    \caption{General topographic map of Australia with outlined location of the study area. Mapping software: Generic Mapping Tools (GMT) version 6.4.0. Data source: GEBCO. Map source: author.}
    \label{fig01}
\end{figure}

One of the most prominent features in the environment of southern Victoria is the coastal plains of Port Phillip Bay -- a largest coastal lagoon system in Victoria, Australia. Port Phillipe Bay is a shallow embayment formed between the end of the last Ice Age around 8000 BCE as a result of faulting and marine transgression. Nowadays, it is located in a shallow tectonic depression with the depths mostly less than 8 m \cite{Holdgate}. With the total area of $1930 km^2$, it presents a closed complex ecosystem with irregular coastline and varying topographic setting and geomorphology \cite{longmore1996nutrient}. 

Landscape formation in this area is driven by diverse factors such as wind force, and strong effects of oceanic waves, tides, and currents. The variability of such processes affects nearby landscapes, controls the distribution of the land cover types and regulates ecosystem functioning. As a result, the landscapes surrounding Port Phillip Bay are presented by a complex mixture of landscapes. Besides the distribution of wetlands, the estuaries of the Port Phillip Bay create a dynamic environment which supports rich marine and coastal biodiversity and important fisheries \cite{Jenkins,Weston,harris1996port}.

The region of southern Victoria around the Port Phillip Bay is notable for distribution of wetlands and salt marshes. Recent environmental survey reported 19212 ha of coastal salt marshes 5177 ha of mangroves, and 3227 ha of estuarine wetland in Victoria \cite{Boon}. Among these, the most notable system of wetlands is Cheetham, which occupies up to 420 ha of lagoons, including both artificial (salt marshes) and natural types (mangroves), Figure \ref{fig02}. 

Cheetham Wetlands are distributed on old salt works land on the western part of Port Phillip Bay and include the seasonal and perennial wetlands protected for conservation purposes. Located ca. 20 kilometres southwest of Melbourne \cite{Westonetal2006}, perennial Cheetham wetlands dry occasionally, only during periods with low precipitation \cite{jmse9080898}. However, much of the flora and fauna are not adapted to drying and remain permanently in the areas filled with water. Such settings create unique environmental ecosystem of Cheetham Wetlands with specific species adapted to such conditions of variable water levels. Hydrological and climate setting of the Cheetham Wetlands favour its high environmental value by providing habitat over 200 species of rare birds including migratory species that migrate to the Southern Hemisphere between July and November \cite{Jackson}.

The dominating land cover types around Port Phillipe Bay and southern Victoria include bushland, native shrubland, pastures and grasslands that intersperse with native forests. Arid zones located further from the coastal area are notable for drylands with sparse crops and rare treed vegetation coverage with scattered trees. Lacustrine and moderate dune systems are also found in the surroundings of Port Phillip. The land affected by the anthropogenic activities includes agricultural farmlands, horticulture and irrigated areas, lands used for recreation, as well as built up areas. Those are occupied by cities and settlements, including small towns and urban constructions (roads, industrial facilities, etc.), Figure \ref{fig03}.

    \end{center}
    \caption{General topographic map of Australia with outlined location of the study area. Mapping software: QGIS version 3.34.7 'Prizren'. Data source: Dynamic Land Cover Dataset (DLCD). Map source: author.}

The current environment of Port Phillip Bay has developed by a complex interactions of climate, lithological variability, tectonic-geologic deformation and sea level changes. Nowadays, the land cover types are continue further changing both along the coastline and in the hinterland, which is mainly caused by human activities such as economic development, residential construction and urban sprawl as well as recreational pressures \cite{FultonSmith}. Current environmental issues in this area include anthropogenic threats such as commercial dredging, water pollution and contamination caused by the trace metals, toxic substances, organochlorines and hydrocarbons \cite{PHILLIPS1992200}. Moreover, recent studies also detected changes to the structure and composition of benthic communities and weed invasions in Port Phillip Bay caused by the environmental interactions \cite{CURRIE199936}. Finally, shallow bathymetry of the Port Phillip Bay contributes to the eutrophication \cite{FultonSmith} and marine algae bloom \cite{md18030142} in the selected parts of the embayment. As a result, the overall condition of Port Phillip Bay appears to have deteriorated on a large scale over the recent decades \cite{JUNG201193}.

\section{Materials and Methods}

An approach that integrates RS data and AI algorithms is proposed herein to identify land cover changes through pixel-level mapping for monitoring area of southern area of Victoria state, Melbourne district.

The data source for the land cover types reference were derived from the nationally consistent and thematically comprehensive land cover reference for Australia -- the Dynamic Land Cover Dataset (DLCD), Figure \ref{fig03}. \section{Discussion}

The results of image classification using four different algorithms of AI and ML were compared to identify their effectiveness in GRASS GIS software. This approach enabled to detect the areas experiencing the most intensive land cover changes in southern Victoria, Port Phillip Bay. Pixel-level mapping enhanced the identification of regions requiring flexible methodology and investigation efficiency by focusing on specific areas such as Cheetham Wetlands which are distributed over the southern segment of the study area. 

%----------- section ----------->
\section{Conclusions}

The use of AI and ML technologies in environmental coastal mapping domain is a novel approach that complements and enhances commonly used traditional GIS methods for RS data processing. Hence, in this paper, four algorithms of AI and ML were applied and compared to address the issue of the effective RS data processing.
